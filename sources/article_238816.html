Voltammetric determination of sulfanilamide as a medicinal drug using Fe3O4@CuO-RGO modified screen printed electrode

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Fatemeh Irannezhad
Jamileh Seyed-Yazdi

Department of Physics, Faculty of Science, Vali-e-Asr University of Rafsanjan, Rafsanjan, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.555747.1020
Abstract
Sulfonamides (Such as sulfanilamide) are a kind of broad-spectrum antimicrobial drugs, used in livestock, poultry and aquaculture. Their residues in food may cause potential harm to consumers' health. Therefore, it is necessary to develop a simple, rapid and high-sensitivity detection method. In this investigation, graphite oxide (GO) was synthesized via a modified Hummer’s method to ensure enhanced oxidation efficiency. Iron oxide (Fe₃O₄) nanoparticles were prepared through a co-precipitation technique, followed by the fabrication of the Fe₃O₄@CuO-reduced graphene oxide (RGO) composite via a solvothermal procedure. Comprehensive characterization of the synthesized materials was conducted employing Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR), X-ray Diffraction (XRD), Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy coupled with Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (FE-SEM/EDX), and Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM). These analyses confirmed the successful synthesis of the composite and provided substantive evidence of the proposed core-shell architecture of Fe₃O₄@CuO, highlighting its structural integrity and morphological features. Then, an electrochemical sensing method for detecting sulfonamide was established. Firstly, bare and modified electrode were screened using cyclic voltammetry(CV). The results showed that sulfonamide on magnetite (Fe3O4) @ CuO nanomaterials (CuO) - Reduced Graphene oxide (RGO) modified electrode had the largest response. The linear regression equation for modified electrode was y=0.352x+0.3753 (R2=0.9995) with the detection limit 0.03 µM. In this study, an electrode modification material with high sensitivity to sulfonamide was selected, and a simple, rapid and stable electrochemical detection method was established. This method has good applicability for determining sulfonamide drug residues in urine sample.
