Synergistic investigation of hexagonal α-MnTe phase synthesized via electrochemical deposition technique for photovoltaic application

Pages 112-123

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.575456.1032

Blessing U. Osolobri, Ernest O. Ojegu, Imosobomeh Lucky Ikhioya

Abstract Manganese telluride (MnTe) is another promising p-type semiconductor for photovoltaic (PV) systems, noted for its high absorption coefficient and tunable band gaps. MnTe thin films in this paper, was deposited on the fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) substrate through an electrochemical deposition process. X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to analyze the structure and supported the standard lattice planes with the hexagonal α-MnTe phase with diffraction peaks of between 25.3o and 75.3o. Morphology on the surface indicated that the early deposition produced single islands, which caused a rough surface. Nevertheless, with the deposition duration increased to 40 seconds, the grain coalescence, and a more homogeneous film is formed. Additional optimization of the deposition time of 50 seconds however increased the thickness of the film and the grain size and improved the maximum absorbance within the UV-visible and near-infrared (NIR) spectra. This expansion also increased the light-trapping ability, which is essential in enhancing high density of current during short circuit. Direct band gap is 1.79 eV, and such electrodeposited MnTe films present a high potential in top-cell absorber in tandem solar cell arrangements on a substrate with lower band gaps.

Original Article

Measurement of butylated hydroxyanisol in food samples using carbon electrode modified with Zn-metal organic framework nanostructures

Pages 124-133

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.576354.1034

Asma Baniebrahimi, Hamid Sarhadi

Abstract Butylated hydroxyanisole (BHA) functions as a potent chemical antioxidant which protects food and food packaging materials and animal feed and cosmetics and pharmaceutical products and rubber and petroleum products. For the sensitive detection of BHA, a Zn-metal organic framework nanostructure (Zn-MOF) is used to modify a screen-printed graphite electrode (SPE). The oxidation process of BHA on the modified SPE was significantly electrocatalyzed by the synergistic impact of Zn-MOF. With a linear concentration range of 0.6–190.0 μM, a limit of detection (LOD) of 0.2 μM, and a high sensitivity of 0.0804 μA/μM, BHA was quantitatively detected. For the detection of BHA, the Zn-MOF/SPE sensor showed high stability, repeatability, and reproducibility. Furthermore, the evaluation of the interference effect of several species on BHA detection showed that the sensor's design has high selectivity for BHA. With recoveries ranging from 96.1 to 103.2% and a relative standard deviation (RSD) of 3.6%, the Zn-MOF/SPE sensor was effectively utilized to determine BHA in actual samples.

Synthesis and investigation of structural and magnetic properties of Fe₂O₃/Fe₃O₄/MWCNT ternary nanocomposite: Application for the detection of the medicine drug epinine

Pages 134-144

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.576556.1035

Reihaneh Mollashafiee, Jamileh Seyed-Yazdi, Farnaz Jamadi

Abstract The main objective of this project is to develop a new electrochemical sensor which detects epinephrine. Researchers synthesized the FeO₃/FeO₄/MWCNT ternary nanocomposite through a simple process which they used multiple microscopic and spectroscopic methods to determine its crystallinity and content and shape. The researchers studied how the fabricated nanocomposite performed in electrochemical tests which involved morphine detection after they deposited the material onto a screen-printed graphite electrode (SPGE). The SPGE developed through nanocomposite modification showed better electrocatalytic performance than the unmodified electrode when testing for epinine oxidation. The researchers examined the current sensor (Fe₂O₃/Fe₃O₄/MWCNT/SPE) using differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) under controlled laboratory conditions. The research results showed a linear dynamic range which extended from 1.0 to 200.0 µM together with a limit of detection (LOD) which reached 0.4 µM. The predicted sensitivity was 0.1894 µA/ µM. Researchers used the Fe₃O₃/Fe₃O₄/MWCNT for voltammetric measurements of epinine in urine samples which resulted in recovery rates of approximately 100% for all tested samples.

Plant-mediated green synthesis and characterization of zinc oxide nanoparticle for enhanced antimicrobial activities against Escherichia coli

Pages 145-157

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.579868.1037

Sylvenus Aguree, Nyefene Joe Dare, Samson Abah Abagale

Abstract Medicinal plants have long been a crucial component of traditional medicine, retaining their importance in human health management for centuries. These plants act as a reservoir of bioactive compounds, which are known to possess antimicrobial properties. Despite the availability of various antimicrobial agents, concerns regarding antibiotic resistance remain prevalent. Several strategies are employed to address this issue. In this study, zinc oxide nanoparticles synthesized from Iccacina oliviformis were prepared using an eco-friendly precipitation method. The antimicrobial activities of both the aqueous extract and the synthesized nanoparticles were assessed using minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) methods. Characterization of the prepared zinc oxide nanoparticles was performed using Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Phytochemical analysis identified several constituents, including alkaloids, tannins, saponins, flavonoids, terpenoids, and anthraquinones. The ZnO NPs synthesized from Iccacina oliviformis demonstrated stronger antimicrobial activity, with an MIC of 0.98 µg/mL, compared to 7.8 µg/mL for the aqueous extract of Iccacina oliviformis root tuber alone. FTIR analysis revealed the presence of alcohols, carbonyls, alkenes, and nitro-compounds, phenols, functional groups originating from O-H, C=O, C–C, N=O, and C-H. The XRD analysis indicated a hexagonal wurtzite phase of ZnO, while SEM images displayed nano-sized structures characterized by agglomeration and high surface roughness. The formation of ZnO nanoparticles was clearly observed in this study, and antimicrobial assays confirmed that the synthesized ZnO nanoparticles exhibited inhibitory effects against Escherichia coli. Further research is needed to investigate the potential toxicities associated with the synthesized nanoparticles.

Original Article

Electrochemical sensor based on modified carbon paste electrode for isoniazid detection

Pages 158-170

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.579912.1038

Mahdiyeh Sheikhshoaei, Marziyeh Behzadpuor, Iran Sheikhshoaie

Abstract Isoniazid (ISD) is a commonly prescribed antibiotic medication used to treat tuberculosis. To detect isoniazid (ISD), a carbon paste electrode (ZnFe2O4/CPE) was modified using ZnFe2O4 nanostructures. Analysis revealed that ISD electro-oxidation had a remarkable catalytic activity, leading to a significant peak potential decrease (~130 mV) for ISD. The electrochemical characteristics of the modified electrode for the ISD detection were characterized using chronoamperometry, differential pulse voltammetry (DPV), linear sweep voltammogram (LSV), and cyclic voltammetry (CV). The peak current of oxidation and the different levels of ISD (0.1–300.0 µM) showed a linear relationship under ideal conditions, with a narrow detection limit of 0.04±0.001 µM. By measuring the ISD in tablets and urine specimens, the as-developed sensor's applicability was verified with respectable recoveries.

Original Article

A novel nano complex of Zn (II) with turmeric: Synthesis, characterization, spectroscopic methods, and interaction with FS-DNA

Pages 171-185

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.581553.1039

Najme Rezaei, Niloufar Akbarzadeh-T

Abstract This study examined a zinc (II) ion nano-complex with curcumin (turmeric) ligand. X-ray scattering, scanning electron microscopy, and UV-Vis and Fourier infrared spectroscopies were used to identify the nanoparticles after they were generated using the ultrasonic probe approach. The scanning electron microscope's images demonstrated the particles' consistent shape. The size of the particles was less than 100 nm, according to calculations based on the model, which is evidence that nanoparticles were generated. Lastly, fluorescence, UV-Vis, and gel electrophoresis techniques were used to study the interaction of nanoparticle (a) with fish salmon DNA (FS-DNA). These interactions were also used to derive the thermodynamic values ΔH°, ΔS°, and ΔG°. The results demonstrated how hydrogen bonds and van der Waals forces contribute to the groove binding form of interaction between the DNA and the nano-complex (a). The finding that larger concentrations of the complex had no effect on the viscosity of the DNA was particularly intriguing since it suggests that the binding interaction did not significantly disrupt the helical structure of the DNA. Circular dichroism (CD) spectra revealed that the interaction with the nano-complex significantly altered the DNA structure (a). This discovery highlights the necessity of using multiple analytical tools to successfully distinguish between metal-DNA interactions and shows how complicatedly they subtly affect DNA structure.
