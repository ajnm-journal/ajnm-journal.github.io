Applications of chemically modified electrodes in norepinephrine analysis: A review

Abstract
Norepinephrine (NEP) is classified as a catecholamine, a neurotransmitter that is essential to human function. Catecholamines in biological samples must be measured precisely and selectively to be used for clinical diagnosis and the study of certain pathological disorders. Modification of the working electrode surface is often employed to achieve rapid, accurate, selective, and sensitive detection of NEP quickly, accurately, selectively, and sensitively. Several research approaches that have effectively identified NEP utilizing surface-modified electrodes in various genuine samples have been carefully reviewed in this work. With a variety of modified electrodes, such as glassy carbon, carbon paste, screen-printed electrodes, and those augmented with carbon nanotubes (CNTs), the electrochemical detection of NEP is the main focus. The analytical performance indicators of several research methodologies for NEP detection are also covered.
