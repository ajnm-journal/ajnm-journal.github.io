Biochemistry techniques in diabetes disease studies

Abstract
Diabetes mellitus is a prevalent metabolic disease that causes many complications when it is poorly managed in terms of blood glucose control. Diagnosing diabetes and its complications require accurate measuring of biochemical and molecular markers of diseases. Recently, several biochemical approaches have been developed to identify proteins, antibodies, cytokines, hormones, and other disease markers in biological samples. The present review provides a brief description of major biochemical approaches employed for investigating diabetes with special attention paid to four approaches emphasized in this review, namely enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), Luminex, single-molecule array (SiMoA), and agglutination-PCR (ADAP). Overall, the discussed biochemical approaches offer complementary information regarding development of diabetes and its complications. Increased sensitivity, ability for multiplexing, and early biomarker detection of discussed biochemical approaches may advance the field of diabetes research. Studies included in the present paper are devoted to different types of research on diabetes but all of them use one of the discussed biochemical approaches as the detection method.
