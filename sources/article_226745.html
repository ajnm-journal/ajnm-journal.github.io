Swcnt-based nanocarriers for improved pharmacokinetics of omacetaxine mepesuccinate in hematological malignancies

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Mahsa Malekpour 1
Mehdi Yoosefian 1, 2

1 Department of Nanotechnology, Graduate University of Advanced Technology, Kerman, Iran

2 Department of Chemisry, Graduate University of Advanced Technology, Kerman, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.532542.1002
Abstract
 Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) is a myeloproliferative neoplasm driven by the BCR-ABL1 fusion oncogene, for which tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have significantly improved patient outcomes. However, the emergence of TKI resistance and the pharmacological limitations of alternative agents such as omacetaxine mepesuccinate—namely, poor solubility, rapid metabolism, and dose-limiting toxicities—underscore the need for advanced drug delivery strategies. This study investigates the encapsulation dynamics and molecular interactions of omacetaxine mepesuccinate within single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) as a potential nanocarrier platform, using state-of-the-art molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. A capped, armchair SWCNT (4.0 nm length, 1.3 nm diameter) and an optimized omacetaxine molecule were constructed and parameterized using the CHARMM36 force field. The SWCNT/omacetaxine complex was simulated in explicit aqueous solution under physiological conditions for 200 ns using GROMACS 2024.5. Analyses included assessments of system stability, drug encapsulation efficiency, interaction energy profiles, conformational dynamics, and solvent accessibility. MD simulations revealed spontaneous encapsulation and persistent confinement of omacetaxine within the SWCNT cavity, driven predominantly by strong van der Waals and π–π interactions, with negligible electrostatic contributions. The complex exhibited remarkable structural and energetic stability, as evidenced by stable potential energy, Root Mean Square Deviation, and radius of gyration values throughout the simulation.
