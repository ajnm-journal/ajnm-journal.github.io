Synthesis of MoO₃ nanoparticles and its application to construct an electrochemical sensor for determination of hydroxylamine

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Razieh Razavi 1
Ali Ahmadi 2

1 Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Science, University of Jiroft, Iran

2 Department of Chemistry, Ke.C., Islamic Azad University, Kerman, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.533409.1004
Abstract
In this study, MoO3 nanoparticles (MoO3 NPs) were fabricated through a straightforward technique. Confirmation of their formation was provided by FT-IR, XRD, FE-SEM, and EDX. Subsequently, an electronic sensing device, which incorporated a pre-designed graphite-based electrode (SPGE) combined with MoO3 NPs, was introduced for the analysis of hydroxylamine presence in aqueous solutions. The combined MoO3 NPs and SPGE setup exhibited remarkable efficacy in detecting hydroxylamine, a phenomenon supported by cyclic voltammetry (CV) data. A unique oxidation signature without a corresponding reduction in the reverse scan was observed, revealing the one-way nature of hydroxylamine's electrochemical oxidation on both the standard SPGE and the combined MoO3/SPGE setup. When conditions were optimized (pH and pulsing specifics), a consistent correlation with its concentration, spanning from 0.04 µM to 675.0 µM and having a sensitivity threshold (LOD) of 0.01 µM, was exhibited by the voltammetric reaction of hydroxylamine on the MoO3/SPGE. Moreover, the real-world relevance of the MoO3/SPGE sensing apparatus was assessed by measuring hydroxylamine levels in water sources. Data highlighted recuperation metrics from 96.7% to 104.3% and consistency measures (RSDs) at or less than 3.1% for both river and domestic water specimens.
