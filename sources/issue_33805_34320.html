Original Article

Glassy carbon electrode modified with Fe3O4/CuS/PANI/RGO nanostructure for efficient electrochemical sensing of mangiferin

Pages 186-198

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.579143.1036

Fatemeh Irannezhad, Jamileh Seyed-Yazdi

Abstract A Fe3O4/CuS/PANI/RGO nanostructure was created for this investigation. Several methods were used to examine the structure and morphology of the Fe3O4/CuS/PANI/RGO nanostructure, and then as a working electrode for the voltammetric detection of mangiferin (MANG), a glassy carbon electrode (GCE) modified with a manufactured nanostructure (Fe3O4/CuS/PANI/RGO/GCE) was used. The Fe3O4/CuS/PANI/RGO/GCE showed superior electro-catalytic activity for MANG oxidation as compared to unmodified GCE. The limit of detection (LOD) is 0.5±0.02 μM, and under ideal circumstances, the oxidation peak currents of MANG show a linear relationship with its concentration in the range of 1.0–250.0 μM. Lastly, MANG analysis was used to confirm the constructed sensor's applicability in real samples, yielding outstanding results (recovery 98.0–104.3%, and relative standard deviation (RSD) < 3.4%).

Review Article

Modified voltammetric sensors for measurement of methyl parathion

Pages 199-215

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.583321.1040

Niloufar Akbarzadeh-T

Abstract Population growth of humans leads to expansion of industrial agriculture in which pesticides are an essential component. These chemicals allow the destruction of harmful factors such as weeds, insects, and microbes; that not only damage the health value of foods, but also cause unpleasant effects on human beings. Hence, detection, and quantification of pesticides are vital issues concerning human beings’ health. Since the development of numerous analytical chemical methods for the determination of these organic compounds, electrochemical techniques, considering their sensitivity, selectivity, low cost, and environmental friendliness have received wide interest recently. This paper reviews the research papers that concern the construction and performance of modified electrochemical voltammetric sensors to determine and quantify pesticides.

Molecular dynamics simulation and conformational stability analysis of HPV-16 E6 oncoprotein–ligand complexes: cilostazol as a promising stable candidate

Pages 216-228

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.584345.1041

Mahshid Ahmadi

Abstract Persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus type 16 (HPV-16) is a primary driver of cervical cancer and several other malignancies. The viral E6 oncoprotein promotes oncogenesis by mediating the degradation of the tumor suppressor p53. Therefore, compounds capable of maintaining stable interactions with E6 may provide a rational basis for antiviral and anticancer drug development. In this study, the dynamic stability of five selected ligands (axitinib, cabozantinib, tivozanib, deflazacort, and cilostazol) in complex with HPV-16 E6 was evaluated through 300-ns all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations using GROMACS 2022.4, the CHARMM36 force field, and the TIP3P water model. Structural stability, flexibility, compactness, and interaction persistence were assessed using RMSD, RMSF, hydrogen bonding, radius of gyration (Rg), protein–ligand distance, contact number, and solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) analyses. Among the five complexes, the cilostazol–E6 system exhibited the most favorable overall dynamic stability profile throughout the 300-ns simulation. It maintained 6–10 persistent hydrogen bonds, a stable radius of gyration (~1.70 nm), a consistent protein–ligand distance (~0.18 nm), and a protein–ligand contact profile that increased from approximately 800–1200 contacts during the early stage to 1500–2000 contacts during the later phase of the simulation, along with an average SASA of approximately 97 nm², collectively reflecting sustained structural integrity and interaction persistence. In comparison, the other ligands (axitinib, cabozantinib, tivozanib, and deflazacort) generally exhibited less favorable dynamic behavior across the evaluated molecular dynamics parameters, indicating lower overall complex stability. These findings demonstrate that cilostazol forms a more stable complex with HPV-16 E6 than the other tested compounds and represents a promising candidate for further experimental investigation as a potential E6-targeted antiviral and anticancer agent.

Original Article

Nanomedicine-induced transcriptomic biomarkers in gastrointestinal cancers

Pages 229-240

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.584690.1044

Morteza Hadizadeh, Nahid Askari

Abstract Gastrointestinal cancers (GIC) are leading causes of cancer mortality with limited therapeutic options. Nanomedicine offers targeted delivery, reduced toxicity, and enhanced efficacy in GIC; however, the molecular response to these therapies, particularly nanoparticle therapy, remains unclear. This study analyzed three RNA-Seq datasets representing three therapeutic modalities: Ramucirumab (an anti-VEGFR2 monoclonal antibody) in gastric cancer; Anti-miR-17 (an anti-miRNA oligonucleotide) in hepatocellular carcinoma; and β-cyclodextrin-EDTA-Fe₃O₄ nanoparticles (CPT-CEF) in colorectal cancer. Among these, only CPT-CEF represents a nanoparticle-based nanomedicine. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified via DESeq2 (|log₂FC|≥1, adj.P≤0.05), followed by Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis. Treatment with these modalities, particularly the CPT-CEF nanoparticle formulation, significantly modulated metabolic pathways (carbon metabolism, purine/pyrimidine metabolism, and oxidative phosphorylation), DNA replication, and ribosome biogenesis. A core set of genes (MCM2, MCM5, MCM7, POLA2, FEN1, RNASEH2A, POLD1, POLD2, RNASEH1, EPHX2, RRM2, CCNA2, PLK1, CHEK1, and TOP2A) was consistently dysregulated in GIC and reversed upon treatment. These genes regulate cell cycle progression, DNA repair, and metabolic reprogramming, serving as promising biomarkers for therapy efficacy, particularly for nanoparticle-based treatment, and potential targets for nanocarrier-based delivery. The findings provide a molecular rationale for smart nanotherapeutics, aligning with nanomaterials and medicinal chemistry. This in silico strategy offers a cost-effective approach to identify novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets, accelerating personalized therapy, including nanomedicine, for GIC.

Original Article

A modified pencil electrode based on binary transition-metal sulfide nanoparticles for sensitive detection of the herbicide Diuron

Pages 241-249

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.584389.1042

Amirkhosro Beheshti-Marnani, Tahereh Rohani

Abstract The development of sensitive analytical methods with extremely low detection limits has consistently remained a critical challenge in the control and monitoring of herbicide residues in agricultural food products. In this regard, transition metal sulfide nanostructures can make a significant contribution to the development of effective electrochemical sensors to address this challenge. Needle-shaped CuₓBi₂₋ₓS₃ nanoparticles were employed as an effective electrocatalyst for the sensitive determination of trace amounts of the herbicide Diuron using a graphite pencil based electrochemical sensor. The proposed sensor was successfully applied for the determination of Diuron over a concentration range of 40 to 900 µg/L, with a limit of detection of 17 µg/L and a sensitivity of 0.251 µA·L·µg⁻¹, without any significant interference from coexisting species. Differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) was utilized as the analytical method, and the structure of the synthesized nanocomposite was confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX).

Review Article

Biochemistry techniques in diabetes disease studies

Pages 250-273

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.587461.1048

Raed Muslim Mhaibes, Uzma Asif, Ahmed Elzainy, Abir El Sadik, Parmida Amiri

Abstract Diabetes mellitus is a prevalent metabolic disease that causes many complications when it is poorly managed in terms of blood glucose control. Diagnosing diabetes and its complications require accurate measuring of biochemical and molecular markers of diseases. Recently, several biochemical approaches have been developed to identify proteins, antibodies, cytokines, hormones, and other disease markers in biological samples. The present review provides a brief description of major biochemical approaches employed for investigating diabetes with special attention paid to four approaches emphasized in this review, namely enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), Luminex, single-molecule array (SiMoA), and agglutination-PCR (ADAP). Overall, the discussed biochemical approaches offer complementary information regarding development of diabetes and its complications. Increased sensitivity, ability for multiplexing, and early biomarker detection of discussed biochemical approaches may advance the field of diabetes research. Studies included in the present paper are devoted to different types of research on diabetes but all of them use one of the discussed biochemical approaches as the detection method.
