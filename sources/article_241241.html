Electrochemical detection of methionine using zeolitic imidazolate framework, cobalt ferrite, ionic liquid, and ferrocene monocaroxylic acid modified carbon paste electrode

Abstract
Methionine is a vitamin soluble in water which is found in numerous natural species. Human body eventually runs into problems with the absence of methionine, thus a method that is easy and sensitive for detecting methionine is desired. A new electrode that has been chemically modified was meant for methionine measurement in real samples using zeolitic imidazolate framework (ZIF-67), cobalt ferrite (CoFe2O4), ionic liquid (IL), and Ferrocene monocaroxylic acid (FMC) modified carbon paste electrode (CPE). ZIF-67@CoFe2O4/IL/FMC/CPE was analyzed through scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The parameters of pH and scan rate (ν) were studied by cyclic voltammetry, and the optimized values were selected. The electrochemical parameters, such as diffusion coefficient of methionine (DMethionine), electrode surface area (A), and electron transfer coefficient (α) were determined. For quantitative computations, the accuracy of square wave voltammetry was utilized. A two-linear relation was discovered between anodic peak current (ipa) and methionine concentration (CMethionine) in the 0.1-10.0 µM and 10.0-600.0 µM1 ranges; the limit of detection (LOD) was thus set at 0.03 µM, which is on par with prior research. A novel, simple, quick, and selective sensor was examined to determine methionine in real samples.
