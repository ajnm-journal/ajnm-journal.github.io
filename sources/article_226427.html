Molecular dynamics-informed design of functionalized graphene oxide nanocarriers for enhanced romidepsin delivery in solid tumor therapy

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Sarah Gavari-Bami 1
Hedieh Bayramzadeh 2
Mehdi Yoosefian 1, 2

1 Department of Nanotechnology, Graduate University of Advanced Technology, Kerman, Iran

2 Department of Chemisry, Graduate University of Advanced Technology, Kerman, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.226427
Abstract
Romidepsin (ROM) is a cyclic peptide and FDA-approved histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor with strong antitumor activity in hematological malignancies. However, its clinical application in solid tumors is limited due to poor aqueous solubility, rapid systemic clearance, and unfavorable bio-distribution. We performed 200-nanosecond all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations under physiologically relevant conditions to investigate the ROM interaction with a hydroxyl- and carboxyl-functionalized graphene oxide (GRP) nanosheet. The system was analyzed using metrics including root mean square deviation (RMSD), interaction energy decomposition, hydrogen bonding, and solvent-accessible surface area (SASA). ROM exhibited rapid and stable adsorption onto the GRP surface, primarily driven by van der Waals and π–π stacking interactions, with further stabilization via hydrogen bonding. ROM molecules formed stable, multilayered aggregates on GRP without significant structural disruption, maintaining close surface contact throughout the trajectory. SASA analysis showed increased aqueous exposure and dispersibility of the ROM–GRP complex relative to GRP alone. The results demonstrate the high-affinity, stable loading of ROM onto functionalized GRP, providing atomistic insights into the encapsulation and solubilization mechanism. These findings provide a theoretical framework for the development of GRP-based nanocarriers to improve ROM delivery in solid tumor therapy and support further in vitro and in vivo validation of this nanoplatform for biomedical applications.
