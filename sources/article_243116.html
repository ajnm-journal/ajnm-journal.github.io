Voltammetric determination of catechol in food ingredients using screen printed electrode modified with Fe3O4 nanoparticles

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Habibe Gilani
Batul Rezaei Zadeh
Hamid Sarhadi

Department of Food Science, Bam.C., Islamic Azad University, Bam, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.576133.1033
Abstract
CTC serves as an industrial precursor which manufacturers use to create various products including flavors, pesticides, scents, plastics, herbicides, dyes, tanning agents, rubber, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, preservatives, antioxidants, lubricating oils, and stabilizer materials. Fe3O4 nanoparticles were created here utilizing a straightforward method and are employed as a voltammetric sensing platform. Fe3O4 nanoparticles provide better sensor performance. The sensor was created by applying a suspension of Fe3O4 nanoparticles to a screen-printed carbon electrode (Fe3O4/SPCE) using a simple drop-casting technique. The catechol (CTC) was then measured using a differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) technique using the Fe3O4/SPCE sensor. The Fe3O4 nanoparticles/SPCE sensor showed a high sensitivity of 0.117 µA/µM and a linear response to CTC throughout a concentration range of 1.0 to 300.0 µM. 0.4 µM was the limit of detection (LOD) attained using the DPV technique. The researchers successfully tested the Fe3O4/SPCE sensor for CTC detection in a real sample which resulted in recovery rates between 96.0% and 102.0%.
