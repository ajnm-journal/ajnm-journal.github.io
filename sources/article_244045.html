A novel nano complex of Zn (II) with turmeric: Synthesis, characterization, spectroscopic methods, and interaction with FS-DNA

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Najme Rezaei
Niloufar Akbarzadeh-T

Department of Chemistry, University of Sistan and Baluchestan, P.O. Box 98135-674, Zahedan, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.581553.1039
Abstract
This study examined a zinc (II) ion nano-complex with curcumin (turmeric) ligand. X-ray scattering, scanning electron microscopy, and UV-Vis and Fourier infrared spectroscopies were used to identify the nanoparticles after they were generated using the ultrasonic probe approach. The scanning electron microscope's images demonstrated the particles' consistent shape. The size of the particles was less than 100 nm, according to calculations based on the model, which is evidence that nanoparticles were generated. Lastly, fluorescence, UV-Vis, and gel electrophoresis techniques were used to study the interaction of nanoparticle (a) with fish salmon DNA (FS-DNA). These interactions were also used to derive the thermodynamic values ΔH°, ΔS°, and ΔG°. The results demonstrated how hydrogen bonds and van der Waals forces contribute to the groove binding form of interaction between the DNA and the nano-complex (a). The finding that larger concentrations of the complex had no effect on the viscosity of the DNA was particularly intriguing since it suggests that the binding interaction did not significantly disrupt the helical structure of the DNA. Circular dichroism (CD) spectra revealed that the interaction with the nano-complex significantly altered the DNA structure (a). This discovery highlights the necessity of using multiple analytical tools to successfully distinguish between metal-DNA interactions and shows how complicatedly they subtly affect DNA structure.
