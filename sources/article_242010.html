Emerging role of endophytic fungi in nano-medicine: A review

Document Type : Review Article

Abstract
Medicinal plants are natural sources of therapeutic compounds and play crucial roles in drug discovery. The analysis of phytochemicals, both in terms of quality and quantity, plays a pivotal role in defining a plant's nutritional, pharmaceutical, and biological characteristics. In addition to secondary metabolites, certain microorganisms, referred to as endophytes, have co-evolved with their host plants and produce metabolites similar to those of the host plant, which are of therapeutic value. Among eukaryotic organisms, fungi are a significant group known for their capacity to produce diverse compounds, some of which have direct applications as medicines or in synthetic processes. Myco-nanobiotechnology is a rapidly growing research field that has significantly contributed to the development of novel opportunities and applications in nanomedicine. This breakthrough provides a new approach for producing effective compounds with effects similar to those of natural medicines extracted from plants. This review aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the exciting field of endophytic fungi-mediated synthesis of nanoparticles (NPs) and their pharmacological applications. By addressing the challenges and future perspectives, this review will contribute to the advancement of nanotechnology in medicine and pave the way for innovative therapeutic interventions.
