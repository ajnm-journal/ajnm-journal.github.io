Electrochemical determination of medicinal drug gliclazide using carbon paste electrode modified with Fe3O4/GO nanocomposites

Pages 1-16

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.559156.1021

Fatemeh Irannezhad, Jamileh Seyed-Yazdi

Abstract This study presents the solvothermal synthesis of a FeO₄@CuO core-shell nanostructure. The synthesized materials were extensively characterized to determine their chemical bonding, element composition, and macro, and microstructure. To characterize the nanostructures, advanced analytical characterization methods were used, including Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR), X-ray Diffraction (XRD), Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM), and Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy (FE-SEM/EDX). The successful synthesis of the core-shell structure was unequivocally confirmed through these characterization techniques, which also provided insight into the morphology of the synthesized nanostructures along with structural support. Afterward, an electrochemical sensor based on a modified carbon paste electrode, using FeO₄@CuO core-shell nanostructure, was developed for detecting gliclazide. The modified electrode showed effective behavior in gliclazide electrocatalysis, an experimental sensor created using Fe3O4/GO/CPE compared to bare CPE shows a significant decrease in peak potentials and an increase in sensor sensitivities. The sensor was used to detect gliclazide using differential pulse voltammetry. A detection limit of 0.03 µM (S/N=3) was identified, and a linear correspondence of response currents and gliclazide concentrations was found over the broader concentration range of 1.0-700.0 µM. The study findings suggest that Fe3O4/GO/CPE presents an effective voltametric sensor for future analyses. The sensors showed the ability to effectively detect gliclazide in real samples with high sensitivity.

Application of metal-organic framework-based nanocomposites as drug delivery systems for doxorubicin anticancer drug

Pages 17-34

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.567575.1027

Ahmed Elzainy, Abdallah Alshhab, Hekmah M Jaradat, Mamoon Alokour, Mohamed Aamer Abubaker

Abstract Doxorubicin (DOX), a prominent chemotherapeutic agent from the anthracycline class, is extensively utilized in the management of diverse malignancies, including cervical carcinoma, osteosarcoma, and breast cancer. A major limitation of DOX is its lack of target specificity, leading to significant damage to healthy cells and resulting in a range of adverse side effects. A drug delivery system (DDS) is designed to transport pharmaceutical agents in a controlled and sustained manner, specifically to targeted sites within the body. Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) possess a suite of advantageous characteristics for drug delivery, including high design flexibility, precisely defined porosity, stimuli-responsive behavior, an exceptionally large surface area, and tunable morphology. To date, numerous MOF structures have been extensively investigated and employed as nanocarriers. Their significant appeal in the realm of drug delivery stems from their favorable biocompatibility and exceptional capacity for high drug loading. MOFs, particularly when integrated with complementary materials, have been widely explored as innovative carrier platforms for the delivery of DOX. DDSs function by releasing their active pharmaceutical ingredients in a manner designed to achieve a specific and optimal therapeutic outcome. MOFs serve as a versatile foundational platform for drug delivery, often integrated into nanocomposite materials such as MOF-polymer nanocomposites, MOF-metal oxide nanocomposites, and MOF-graphene oxide nanocomposites. Herein, a summary of MOF nanocomposite-based DDSs for the delivery of DOX is provided over the last eight years.

Electrochemical detection of methionine using zeolitic imidazolate framework, cobalt ferrite, ionic liquid, and ferrocene monocaroxylic acid modified carbon paste electrode

Pages 35-46

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.565446.1026

Mohamad T. Raad, Sameer Abdullah Mutahar Al Ans, Rachid Makhloufi, Ahmed Elzainy, Abir El Sadik, Mohammadamin Sami Sajjadifar

Abstract Methionine is a vitamin soluble in water which is found in numerous natural species. Human body eventually runs into problems with the absence of methionine, thus a method that is easy and sensitive for detecting methionine is desired. A new electrode that has been chemically modified was meant for methionine measurement in real samples using zeolitic imidazolate framework (ZIF-67), cobalt ferrite (CoFe2O4), ionic liquid (IL), and Ferrocene monocaroxylic acid (FMC) modified carbon paste electrode (CPE). ZIF-67@CoFe2O4/IL/FMC/CPE was analyzed through scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The parameters of pH and scan rate (ν) were studied by cyclic voltammetry, and the optimized values were selected. The electrochemical parameters, such as diffusion coefficient of methionine (DMethionine), electrode surface area (A), and electron transfer coefficient (α) were determined. For quantitative computations, the accuracy of square wave voltammetry was utilized. A two-linear relation was discovered between anodic peak current (ipa) and methionine concentration (CMethionine) in the 0.1-10.0 µM and 10.0-600.0 µM1 ranges; the limit of detection (LOD) was thus set at 0.03 µM, which is on par with prior research. A novel, simple, quick, and selective sensor was examined to determine methionine in real samples.

Development of an electrochemical sensor based on ferrocene dicarboxylic acid/Ce-doped SnO2 nanosheets-modified carbon paste electrode for epinine detection

Pages 47-56

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.570658.1030

Sarvin Mohammadi Aghdam, Igor Baklanov, Sergey Povetkin, Andrey Blinov, Valery Varavka

Abstract A carbon paste electrode (CPE) that was modified with ferrocene dicarboxylic acid (FDC) and Ce-doped SnO2 nanosheets (Ce-doped SnO2 NSs) was created for the purpose of efficient epinine detection. Various analytical methods such as field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) and Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) were applied to determine the chemical composition and surface morphology of the prepared Ce-doped SnO2nanosheets. Furthermore, the modified electrodes (FDC/Ce-doped SnO2 NSs/CPE) were tested in a pH range of 2 to 9 for their respective activities towards epinine detection. The electrochemical technique was applied for the determination of the range and limit of detection. The modified electrode exhibited a linear detection range of 0.1-300.0 µM. Moreover, the LODs for low and high doses were equal to 0.03±0.001 µM, respectively. Among the kinetic properties of epinine in different media, diffusion and transport coefficients were studied. Finally, the modified electrode was tested for its activity on an actual sample.

Review Article

Emerging role of endophytic fungi in nano-medicine: A review

Pages 57-83

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.568010.1028

Swati Jaast, Mohit Kumar, Anil Kumar

Abstract Medicinal plants are natural sources of therapeutic compounds and play crucial roles in drug discovery. The analysis of phytochemicals, both in terms of quality and quantity, plays a pivotal role in defining a plant's nutritional, pharmaceutical, and biological characteristics. In addition to secondary metabolites, certain microorganisms, referred to as endophytes, have co-evolved with their host plants and produce metabolites similar to those of the host plant, which are of therapeutic value. Among eukaryotic organisms, fungi are a significant group known for their capacity to produce diverse compounds, some of which have direct applications as medicines or in synthetic processes. Myco-nanobiotechnology is a rapidly growing research field that has significantly contributed to the development of novel opportunities and applications in nanomedicine. This breakthrough provides a new approach for producing effective compounds with effects similar to those of natural medicines extracted from plants. This review aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the exciting field of endophytic fungi-mediated synthesis of nanoparticles (NPs) and their pharmacological applications. By addressing the challenges and future perspectives, this review will contribute to the advancement of nanotechnology in medicine and pave the way for innovative therapeutic interventions.

Review Article

Recent progress in graphitic carbon nitride-based systems for photocatalytic antibiotics degradation: A review

Pages 84-102

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.570649.1029

Bahaaldin Rashidzadeh, Mohammad Murwih Alidmat, Alaa Mohammad Al Shehab, Tamara Shdiefat, Andrey Yurievich Chernov, Igor Rzhepakovsky, Evgeniy Shaposhnikov, Farzaneh Fazeli

Abstract The widespread use of antibiotics in both human healthcare and animal husbandry has led to their persistent presence in various aquatic ecosystems. The accelerating spread of antimicrobial resistance is closely linked to an emerging worldwide health emergency, where a rising number of infectious illnesses are no longer responsive to standard treatment regimens. In response, a variety of approaches have recently been developed to remediate antibiotic contamination. In this context, photodegradation offers a highly promising method for tackling this escalating problem, enabling the complete degradation of antibiotics into harmless byproducts. This technology represents a promising, environmentally safe, and cost-effective approach for the removal of various antibiotics. Graphitic carbon nitride (g-C₃N₄) is widely regarded as a highly promising material for photocatalysis, particularly in applications like environmental cleanup through the removal of organic pollutants. g-C₃N₄-based photocatalysts responsive to visible or sunlight have been extensively investigated for antibiotic breakdown, benefiting from their strong chemical and thermal stability, adjustable photophysical characteristics, and straightforward synthesis routes. This review focuses on the use of diverse g-C₃N₄-based photocatalysts in degrading a range of antibiotics—including sulfonamides, tetracyclines, quinolones, and others—with particular emphasis on their degradation efficiency under varying reaction parameters such as light sources and reaction conditions.

Original Article

Voltammetric determination of catechol in food ingredients using screen printed electrode modified with Fe3O4 nanoparticles

Pages 103-111

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.576133.1033

Habibe Gilani, Batul Rezaei Zadeh, Hamid Sarhadi

Abstract CTC serves as an industrial precursor which manufacturers use to create various products including flavors, pesticides, scents, plastics, herbicides, dyes, tanning agents, rubber, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, preservatives, antioxidants, lubricating oils, and stabilizer materials. Fe3O4 nanoparticles were created here utilizing a straightforward method and are employed as a voltammetric sensing platform. Fe3O4 nanoparticles provide better sensor performance. The sensor was created by applying a suspension of Fe3O4 nanoparticles to a screen-printed carbon electrode (Fe3O4/SPCE) using a simple drop-casting technique. The catechol (CTC) was then measured using a differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) technique using the Fe3O4/SPCE sensor. The Fe3O4 nanoparticles/SPCE sensor showed a high sensitivity of 0.117 µA/µM and a linear response to CTC throughout a concentration range of 1.0 to 300.0 µM. 0.4 µM was the limit of detection (LOD) attained using the DPV technique. The researchers successfully tested the Fe3O4/SPCE sensor for CTC detection in a real sample which resulted in recovery rates between 96.0% and 102.0%.
