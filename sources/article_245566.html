Nanomedicine-induced transcriptomic biomarkers in gastrointestinal cancers

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Morteza Hadizadeh 1
Nahid Askari 2

1 Physiology Research Center, Institute of Neuropharmacology, Kerman University of Medical Sciences, Kerman, Iran

2 Department of Biotechnology, Institute of Science and High Technology and Environmental Sciences, Graduate University of Advanced Technology, Kerman, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.584690.1044
Abstract
Gastrointestinal cancers (GIC) are leading causes of cancer mortality with limited therapeutic options. Nanomedicine offers targeted delivery, reduced toxicity, and enhanced efficacy in GIC; however, the molecular response to these therapies, particularly nanoparticle therapy, remains unclear. This study analyzed three RNA-Seq datasets representing three therapeutic modalities: Ramucirumab (an anti-VEGFR2 monoclonal antibody) in gastric cancer; Anti-miR-17 (an anti-miRNA oligonucleotide) in hepatocellular carcinoma; and β-cyclodextrin-EDTA-Fe₃O₄ nanoparticles (CPT-CEF) in colorectal cancer. Among these, only CPT-CEF represents a nanoparticle-based nanomedicine. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified via DESeq2 (|log₂FC|≥1, adj.P≤0.05), followed by Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis. Treatment with these modalities, particularly the CPT-CEF nanoparticle formulation, significantly modulated metabolic pathways (carbon metabolism, purine/pyrimidine metabolism, and oxidative phosphorylation), DNA replication, and ribosome biogenesis. A core set of genes (MCM2, MCM5, MCM7, POLA2, FEN1, RNASEH2A, POLD1, POLD2, RNASEH1, EPHX2, RRM2, CCNA2, PLK1, CHEK1, and TOP2A) was consistently dysregulated in GIC and reversed upon treatment. These genes regulate cell cycle progression, DNA repair, and metabolic reprogramming, serving as promising biomarkers for therapy efficacy, particularly for nanoparticle-based treatment, and potential targets for nanocarrier-based delivery. The findings provide a molecular rationale for smart nanotherapeutics, aligning with nanomaterials and medicinal chemistry. This in silico strategy offers a cost-effective approach to identify novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets, accelerating personalized therapy, including nanomedicine, for GIC.
