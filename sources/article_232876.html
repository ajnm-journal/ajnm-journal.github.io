Functionalized graphene enhances irinotecan binding: An all-atom MD study of –OH/–COOH decorated surfaces

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Arefeh Esmaeili
Mahshid Ahmadi
Zahra Keshavarz

Department of Chemistry, Graduate University of Advanced Technology, Kerman, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.549657.1018
Abstract
The development of efficient nanocarriers for anticancer therapeutics remains a major challenge due to limitations in drug solubility, bioavailability, and systemic toxicity. In this work, all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were employed to investigate the adsorption behavior of the chemotherapeutic agent irinotecan on pristine graphene (GRP) and functionalized graphene (GRP-F) decorated with hydroxyl (–OH) and carboxyl (–COOH) groups. Both systems were simulated for 100 ns under explicit solvent conditions using the CHARMM36 force field. Energy analyses revealed that drug binding to pristine graphene was predominantly driven by van der Waals interactions, whereas the functionalized surface provided additional stabilization through electrostatic interactions and intermittent hydrogen bonding. Root mean square deviation (RMSD) and radius of gyration (Rg) analyses confirmed the stable adsorption of irinotecan on both carriers, with reduced conformational fluctuations in the GRP-F complex. Contact number and center-of-mass distance profiles demonstrated tighter and more persistent drug–carrier associations in the functionalized system. Moreover, solvent accessible surface area (SASA) analysis indicated reduced solvent exposure upon adsorption, while radial distribution functions (RDF) highlighted a significantly higher probability density of irinotecan near GRP-F compared with pristine graphene. Overall, these findings demonstrate that surface functionalization enhances the binding affinity and stability of irinotecan adsorption on graphene, providing a more favorable platform for drug loading and retention. This study offers atomistic-level insights into graphene-based nanocarriers and underscores the potential of functionalized graphene for the design of next-generation delivery systems for chemotherapeutic agents.
