Rational design of CNT-based nanocarriers for cancer therapy: An atomistic perspective on doxorubicin loading with –COOH and –OH groups

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Arefeh Esmaeili
Mahshid Ahmadi

Department of Chemistry, Graduate University of Advanced Technology, Kerman, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.549645.1017
Abstract
Doxorubicin (DOX) is a potent chemotherapeutic agent with broad efficacy against various malignancies; however, its clinical use is restricted by dose-dependent cardiotoxicity and other systemic side effects. Carbon nanotubes (CNTs) have emerged as promising nanocarriers due to their high surface area, tunable surface chemistry, and potential to improve drug delivery efficiency. In this study, the adsorption and stabilization of DOX onto CNTs functionalized with –COOH and –OH groups were systematically examined using all-atom molecular dynamics simulations. Structural and energetic analyses—including potential and total energies, RMSD, contact number, center-of-mass distance, hydrogen bonding, non-bonded interaction energies, radius of gyration (Rg), solvent accessible surface area (SASA), and radial distribution function (RDF)—confirmed the structural stability and energetic consistency of the CNT–DOX complex under physiological conditions. Van der Waals forces were found to dominate the binding process, complemented by electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding interactions that enhance drug retention. The decrease in Rg and SASA indicated the formation of a compact and solvent-shielded structure, while RDF analysis showed preferential localization of DOX within ~1.0 nm of the CNT surface. These findings reveal the superior loading capacity and stability of functionalized CNTs and provide atomistic insights into their potential as efficient, biocompatible nanocarriers for targeted anticancer drug delivery.
