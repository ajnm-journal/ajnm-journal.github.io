Molecular dynamics-informed design of functionalized graphene oxide nanocarriers for enhanced romidepsin delivery in solid tumor therapy

Pages 1-16

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.226427

Sarah Gavari-Bami, Hedieh Bayramzadeh, Mehdi Yoosefian

Abstract Romidepsin (ROM) is a cyclic peptide and FDA-approved histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor with strong antitumor activity in hematological malignancies. However, its clinical application in solid tumors is limited due to poor aqueous solubility, rapid systemic clearance, and unfavorable bio-distribution. We performed 200-nanosecond all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations under physiologically relevant conditions to investigate the ROM interaction with a hydroxyl- and carboxyl-functionalized graphene oxide (GRP) nanosheet. The system was analyzed using metrics including root mean square deviation (RMSD), interaction energy decomposition, hydrogen bonding, and solvent-accessible surface area (SASA). ROM exhibited rapid and stable adsorption onto the GRP surface, primarily driven by van der Waals and π–π stacking interactions, with further stabilization via hydrogen bonding. ROM molecules formed stable, multilayered aggregates on GRP without significant structural disruption, maintaining close surface contact throughout the trajectory. SASA analysis showed increased aqueous exposure and dispersibility of the ROM–GRP complex relative to GRP alone. The results demonstrate the high-affinity, stable loading of ROM onto functionalized GRP, providing atomistic insights into the encapsulation and solubilization mechanism. These findings provide a theoretical framework for the development of GRP-based nanocarriers to improve ROM delivery in solid tumor therapy and support further in vitro and in vivo validation of this nanoplatform for biomedical applications.

Swcnt-based nanocarriers for improved pharmacokinetics of omacetaxine mepesuccinate in hematological malignancies

Pages 17-29

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.532542.1002

Mahsa Malekpour, Mehdi Yoosefian

Abstract  Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) is a myeloproliferative neoplasm driven by the BCR-ABL1 fusion oncogene, for which tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have significantly improved patient outcomes. However, the emergence of TKI resistance and the pharmacological limitations of alternative agents such as omacetaxine mepesuccinate—namely, poor solubility, rapid metabolism, and dose-limiting toxicities—underscore the need for advanced drug delivery strategies. This study investigates the encapsulation dynamics and molecular interactions of omacetaxine mepesuccinate within single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) as a potential nanocarrier platform, using state-of-the-art molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. A capped, armchair SWCNT (4.0 nm length, 1.3 nm diameter) and an optimized omacetaxine molecule were constructed and parameterized using the CHARMM36 force field. The SWCNT/omacetaxine complex was simulated in explicit aqueous solution under physiological conditions for 200 ns using GROMACS 2024.5. Analyses included assessments of system stability, drug encapsulation efficiency, interaction energy profiles, conformational dynamics, and solvent accessibility. MD simulations revealed spontaneous encapsulation and persistent confinement of omacetaxine within the SWCNT cavity, driven predominantly by strong van der Waals and π–π interactions, with negligible electrostatic contributions. The complex exhibited remarkable structural and energetic stability, as evidenced by stable potential energy, Root Mean Square Deviation, and radius of gyration values throughout the simulation.

Fe₃O₄-NH₂/GO an effective modifier for simultaneous voltammetric detection of isoniazid in the presence of acetaminophen

Pages 30-51

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.531917.1000

Fatemeh Ebrahimi-Tazangi, Jamileh Seyed-Yazdi, Hadi Beitollahi

Abstract This study describes a simple and cost-effective procedure to develop a new electrochemical sensor to simultaneously detect acetaminophen (AC) and isoniazid (INZ). We present the study of a screen-printed graphite electrode (SPGE) modified with Fe3O4-NH2/GO nanocomposite by differential pulse voltammetry (DPV), chronoamperometry (CHA), and cyclic voltammetry (CV). Additionally, we used X-ray diffraction (XRD), vibrating sample magnetometry (VSM), Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), and field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) to characterize the synthesized nanocomposites. The DPV peak current of INZ increases linearly with a concentration in the range 0.07-460.0 µM and its limit of detection (LOD) is 0.02±0.001 µM. The results show that the modified electrode exhibits robust functions to circumvent the problem of overlapping the two voltammetric peaks of INZ and AC. We demonstrate the interest in using Fe3O4-NH2/GO/SPGE to detect INZ and AC in real samples.

MOF/MWCNTs nanocomposite modified glassy carbon electrode for voltammetric determination of sulfanilamide

Pages 52-62

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.533392.1003

Zahra Dourandish, Zahra Ghasemia

Abstract Sulfanilamide (SFA) is a synthetic antimicrobial chemical that belongs to the sulfonamides class and is frequently used for both therapeutic and preventative purposes in both human and veterinary medicine. The solvothermal method was used in this study to successfully prepare a nanocomposite material of an iron-based metal-organic framework (MIL-101(Fe)-NH₂) with the addition of multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs), or MIL-101(Fe)-NH₂/MWCNTs. The prepared nanocomposite was then utilized as the working electrode for the voltammetric determination of SFA after modifying a glassy carbon electrode (GCE) to create the MIL-101(Fe)-NH₂/MWCNTs/GCE. The modified electrode demonstrated notable electrocatalytic activity towards the SFA oxidation compared to the bare GCE. Under optimized experimental conditions, the oxidation peak current of SFA increased linearly with concentration between 0.5 and 100.0 μM, with a computed limit of detection of 0.3±0.02 μM.

Antibacterial effects of mixed ligand complex formation of 2-Aminobenzamide with Cu(II) in the presence of glycine

Pages 63-70

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.533607.1005

Niloufar Akbarzadeh-T, Bahareh Karami

Abstract A mixed ligand of bioactive Cu(II) complex has been synthesized from 2aminobenzamide (2AB) and glycine (gly). Then structurally characterized by various physico-chemical and spectral studies such as FT-IR, UV- Vis methods and elemental analysis. The results showed that the 2-aminobenzamide ligand was coordinated to the metal ion via the N and O atoms. Based on the spectral studies, both the ligands act as bidentates and they chelate with Cu(II) ion to form a stable six, five membered chelate ring with octahedral geometry. In this study, inhibition method and dilution method were used to determine the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and the lowest bactericidal concentration (MBC) and the results showed that the synthesized complex against bacteria are active. The antibacterial behavior of the synthesized compound was examined for two groups of gram-negative bacteria (Escherichia coli) and gram-positive (Staphylococus aureus). The results showed that among the synthesized samples complex showed higher antimicrobial activity than ligands.

Original Article

Synthesis of MoO₃ nanoparticles and its application to construct an electrochemical sensor for determination of hydroxylamine

Pages 71-89

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.533409.1004

Razieh Razavi, Ali Ahmadi

Abstract In this study, MoO3 nanoparticles (MoO3 NPs) were fabricated through a straightforward technique. Confirmation of their formation was provided by FT-IR, XRD, FE-SEM, and EDX. Subsequently, an electronic sensing device, which incorporated a pre-designed graphite-based electrode (SPGE) combined with MoO3 NPs, was introduced for the analysis of hydroxylamine presence in aqueous solutions. The combined MoO3 NPs and SPGE setup exhibited remarkable efficacy in detecting hydroxylamine, a phenomenon supported by cyclic voltammetry (CV) data. A unique oxidation signature without a corresponding reduction in the reverse scan was observed, revealing the one-way nature of hydroxylamine's electrochemical oxidation on both the standard SPGE and the combined MoO3/SPGE setup. When conditions were optimized (pH and pulsing specifics), a consistent correlation with its concentration, spanning from 0.04 µM to 675.0 µM and having a sensitivity threshold (LOD) of 0.01 µM, was exhibited by the voltammetric reaction of hydroxylamine on the MoO3/SPGE. Moreover, the real-world relevance of the MoO3/SPGE sensing apparatus was assessed by measuring hydroxylamine levels in water sources. Data highlighted recuperation metrics from 96.7% to 104.3% and consistency measures (RSDs) at or less than 3.1% for both river and domestic water specimens.
