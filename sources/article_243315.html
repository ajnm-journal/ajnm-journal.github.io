Measurement of butylated hydroxyanisol in food samples using carbon electrode modified with Zn-metal organic framework nanostructures

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Asma Baniebrahimi
Hamid Sarhadi

Department of Food Science, Bam. C., Islamic Azad University, Bam, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.576354.1034
Abstract
Butylated hydroxyanisole (BHA) functions as a potent chemical antioxidant which protects food and food packaging materials and animal feed and cosmetics and pharmaceutical products and rubber and petroleum products. For the sensitive detection of BHA, a Zn-metal organic framework nanostructure (Zn-MOF) is used to modify a screen-printed graphite electrode (SPE). The oxidation process of BHA on the modified SPE was significantly electrocatalyzed by the synergistic impact of Zn-MOF. With a linear concentration range of 0.6–190.0 μM, a limit of detection (LOD) of 0.2 μM, and a high sensitivity of 0.0804 μA/μM, BHA was quantitatively detected. For the detection of BHA, the Zn-MOF/SPE sensor showed high stability, repeatability, and reproducibility. Furthermore, the evaluation of the interference effect of several species on BHA detection showed that the sensor's design has high selectivity for BHA. With recoveries ranging from 96.1 to 103.2% and a relative standard deviation (RSD) of 3.6%, the Zn-MOF/SPE sensor was effectively utilized to determine BHA in actual samples.
