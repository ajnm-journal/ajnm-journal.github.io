Original Article

Killing of breast cancer cells by nano-coated graphene oxide: targeting with folic acid and loading with carvacrol

Pages 90-99

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.534103.1006

Fariba Garkani Nejad, Shahryar Shakeri, Hadi Beitollahi

Abstract In this investigation, we developed a graphene oxide (GO) based biocompatible and targeted nanobiocomposite using folic acid (FA) and human serum albumin (HSA) that carvacrol is loaded on graphene sheets. Separately, GO was conjugated to poly l-lysine (PLL) (GO-PLL) and HSA was conjugated to FA (HSA-FA). Then GO-PLL was covalently linked to HSA-FA. SEM showed that GO-PLL sheets were nano-coated by HSA-FA layer (HSA-FA nano-coated GO-PLL). HSA-FA nano-coated GO-PLL was applied for modification of glassy carbon electrode surface. The modified electrode was used for study of MCF7 cancer cells using linear sweep voltammetry method. Results showed a decrease in signal of conjugated folic acid concurrently with increasing the number of MCF7 cells. So, carvacrol was loaded on nano-coated GO-PLL sheets by π-π interactions. Entrapment efficiency (EE%) of carvacrol was 90.8 %. Cytotoxicity effect of carvacrol-loaded nano-coated GO-PLL was evaluated on MCF-7 cell line using the 3-[4,5 dimethylthiazol-2-yl]-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) test. The cells were exposed to the free carvacrol, GO, unloaded-nanobiocomposite, and carvacrol-loaded nano-coated GO-PLL for 48 h. The IC50 of free carvacrol and the carvacrol-loaded nano-coated GO-PLL were determined to be 0.8 mg/ml and 1 mg/ml, respectively. The findings of this study demonstrated the potential of the as-prepared GO-based nanobiocomposite in inducing cytotoxicity against breast cancer cells.

Preparation of bimetallic Cu, Zn complex with OPD: Synthesis, characterization, spectroscopic methods, and interaction with FS-DNA

Pages 100-113

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.534445.1007

Samira Azoogha, Niloufar Akbarzadeh-Tb

Abstract In this study, the bimetallic [Zn(OPD)(μ-SCN)Cu(OPD)] (a), where OPD = o-phenylenediamine ligand, was synthesized. The complex (a) was studied by spectral tools, such as FT-IR and UV-Vis spectroscopy. Interaction studies of complex (a) with fish sperm DNA (FS-DNA) were also conducted using fluorescence, UV-Vis, and gel electrophoresis techniques. Additionally, thermodynamic parameters ΔH°, ΔS°, and ΔG° were calculated from these interactions. The results revealed the hydrogen bonding and van der Waals forces in groove binding mode interaction between the complex (a) and the DNA. Noteworthy, the viscosity measurement showed that increased concentration of the complex has no effect on DNA viscosity, suggesting that binding interaction will not produce much distortion of the helical structure of the DNA. Circular dichroism (CD) spectra revealed extensive alteration in the DNA structure upon interaction of the DNA with the complex (a). This means that while the complex unfolds the DNA in coordination without changing its viscosity, it decreases detectable conformational changes by CD spectroscopy. This discovery in two ways illustrates how complex stealthily manipulates DNA structure and indicates the significance of using more than a single analytical method to efficiently distinguish between metal-DNA interactions.

Theoretical investigation of C20 nanocarrier for fluoxetine in biological media

Pages 114-123

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.534797.1009

Sayed Ali Ahmadi, Razieh Razavi

Abstract Recently, fullerene nanoparticles have garnered significant interest because of their distinct physical and chemical characteristics. When appropriately modified, these nanoparticles exhibit strong biocompatibility, notable anti-tumor effects, and anti-depressant properties, making them promising for cancer and depression treatment. In this study, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were employed to theoretically analyze the interaction between fluoxetine (F), a medicinal compound, and the C20 surface in both gas phase and physiological environments. The DFT results and adsorption energy data indicate that this nanocarrier performs effectively in biological systems.

Original Article

Electrochemical determination of methyldopa using modified screen printed carbon electrode

Pages 114-132

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.534746.1008

Mohammad Reza Aflatoonian, Somayeh Tajik

Abstract This study aimed to design and build an electrochemical sensing platform for measuring methyldopa (MDP). To do this, a nanocomposite of CoWO4 nanoparticles (NPs) anchored to reduced grapheme oxide nanosheets (rGO NSs) was made using a straightforward hydrothermal method. FE-SEM techniques were employed to verify the synthesis of the CoWO4/rGO nanocomposite (CWRGs). The produced CWRGs were then used to modify the surface of a screen-printed carbon electrode (SPCE) to enhance MDP determination. The outstanding electrochemical response of the modified SPCE (CWRGs/SPCE) to the MDP oxidation was investigated using cyclic voltammetry (CV) due to the advantageous properties of CoWO4 NPs and rGO NSs as well as their complimentary effects. For MDP determination, the CWRGs/SPCE sensor showed a low limit of detection (LOD) of 0.1±0.01 μM and a wide linear detection range of 0.3 to 180.0 μM under ideal circumstances. Additionally, CWRGs/SPCE had a sensitivity of 0.1156 μA/μM for MDP detection. Furthermore, the possibility for detecting this molecule in actual samples was demonstrated by the excellent recoveries for detecting spiked MDP in the MDP tablet and urine samples at the CWRGs/SPCE surface.

Carbon nanomaterials-modified electrochemical sensors and their applications in the detection of vitamins B, C, D, and K

Pages 134-146

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.536201.1012

Andrey Nagdalian, Andrey Yurievich Chernov, Igor Rzhepakovsky, Evgeniy Shaposhnikov, Igor Baklanov, Sergey Povetkin, Andrey Blinov, Valery Varavka, Samuel N. Ndung'u, Khaled W. Omari, Bala Ganesh Pichamuthu

Abstract Vitamins are a class of organic substances that are essential to the proper functioning of living organisms. Certain vitamins are considered important because, although they can be produced by these organisms, they must be consumed to meet their needs. The human body may develop metabolic diseases as a result of vitamin deficiencies or insufficiencies; therefore, it is critical to monitor vitamin levels and ensure you get enough of them daily through food or supplements. The determination of vitamins is primarily achieved through various analytical methods, including chromatographic, spectroscopic, and spectrometric techniques. Ongoing research aims to develop new and more efficient methodologies for vitamin analysis, with electroanalytical methods, particularly voltammetry, being among the most common. This study focuses on the use of electroanalytical techniques for vitamin determination, highlighting the advancements in voltammetry developed in recent years. Specifically, this review provides a comprehensive bibliographic survey of modified electrode surfaces utilizing carbon nanomaterials for vitamin analysis.

Original Article

Removal of methylene blue from water samples using multi-walled carbon nanotube/ ZnCo-zeolite imidazole framework nanocomposite

Pages 147-159

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.535708.1011

Sayed Zia Mohammadi, Mahdoght Arjmand Kermani, Fatemeh Emambakhsh

Abstract The ZnCo-Zeolite imidazole framework multi-walled carbon nanotube nanocomposite was prepared for the study. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), X-ray diffractometry (XRD), and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) were used in the characterization of the synthesized nanocomposite. The catalytic performance of the nanocomposite was also examined for its probable adsorptive removal of methylene blue (MB). Experiments were also conducted on the influence of different parameters such as ionic strength, pH, contact time, and initial concentration. Freundlich and Langmuir isotherms were employed to fit the adsorption equilibrium data, and the Langmuir model was the most appropriate. For the MB removal, the maximum sorption capacity was found to be 28.57 mg/g at 298 K. Both pseudo-first order and pseudo-second order diffusion models were employed to determine the adsorption kinetics and the best one was the pseudo-first order model. The as-prepared nanocomposite has high adsorption capacity for MB from water samples based on these data.
