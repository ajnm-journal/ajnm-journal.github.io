Electrochemical sensor based on modified carbon paste electrode for isoniazid detection

Document Type : Original Article

Authors

Mahdiyeh Sheikhshoaei 1, 2
Marziyeh Behzadpuor 2, 3
Iran Sheikhshoaie 2, 3

1 Department of Mining, Faculty of Engineering, Shahid Bahonar University of Kerman, Kerman, Iran

2 Institute of Plant Production (IPP), Afzalipour Research Institute (ARI), Shahid Bahonar University of Kerman, Kerman, Iran

3 Department of Chemistry, Faculty of Sciences, Shahid Bahonar University of Kerman, Kerman, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.579912.1038
Abstract
Isoniazid (ISD) is a commonly prescribed antibiotic medication used to treat tuberculosis. To detect isoniazid (ISD), a carbon paste electrode (ZnFe2O4/CPE) was modified using ZnFe2O4 nanostructures. Analysis revealed that ISD electro-oxidation had a remarkable catalytic activity, leading to a significant peak potential decrease (~130 mV) for ISD. The electrochemical characteristics of the modified electrode for the ISD detection were characterized using chronoamperometry, differential pulse voltammetry (DPV), linear sweep voltammogram (LSV), and cyclic voltammetry (CV). The peak current of oxidation and the different levels of ISD (0.1–300.0 µM) showed a linear relationship under ideal conditions, with a narrow detection limit of 0.04±0.001 µM. By measuring the ISD in tablets and urine specimens, the as-developed sensor's applicability was verified with respectable recoveries.
