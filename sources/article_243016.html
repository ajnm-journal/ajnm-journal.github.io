Abstract
The widespread use of antibiotics in both human healthcare and animal husbandry has led to their persistent presence in various aquatic ecosystems. The accelerating spread of antimicrobial resistance is closely linked to an emerging worldwide health emergency, where a rising number of infectious illnesses are no longer responsive to standard treatment regimens. In response, a variety of approaches have recently been developed to remediate antibiotic contamination. In this context, photodegradation offers a highly promising method for tackling this escalating problem, enabling the complete degradation of antibiotics into harmless byproducts. This technology represents a promising, environmentally safe, and cost-effective approach for the removal of various antibiotics. Graphitic carbon nitride (g-C₃N₄) is widely regarded as a highly promising material for photocatalysis, particularly in applications like environmental cleanup through the removal of organic pollutants. g-C₃N₄-based photocatalysts responsive to visible or sunlight have been extensively investigated for antibiotic breakdown, benefiting from their strong chemical and thermal stability, adjustable photophysical characteristics, and straightforward synthesis routes. This review focuses on the use of diverse g-C₃N₄-based photocatalysts in degrading a range of antibiotics—including sulfonamides, tetracyclines, quinolones, and others—with particular emphasis on their degradation efficiency under varying reaction parameters such as light sources and reaction conditions.
