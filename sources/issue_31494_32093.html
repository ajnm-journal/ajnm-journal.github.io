Review Article

Applications of chemically modified electrodes in norepinephrine analysis: A review

Pages 160-170

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.539638.1015

Zaher Abdel Baki, Khaled W. Omari, Hamad M Adress Hasan, Salma Mohamed Saleh Omar Korbag, Mohamed Salah El-Sayed, Eman K. Mohamed

Abstract Norepinephrine (NEP) is classified as a catecholamine, a neurotransmitter that is essential to human function. Catecholamines in biological samples must be measured precisely and selectively to be used for clinical diagnosis and the study of certain pathological disorders. Modification of the working electrode surface is often employed to achieve rapid, accurate, selective, and sensitive detection of NEP quickly, accurately, selectively, and sensitively. Several research approaches that have effectively identified NEP utilizing surface-modified electrodes in various genuine samples have been carefully reviewed in this work. With a variety of modified electrodes, such as glassy carbon, carbon paste, screen-printed electrodes, and those augmented with carbon nanotubes (CNTs), the electrochemical detection of NEP is the main focus. The analytical performance indicators of several research methodologies for NEP detection are also covered.

A review of recent advances in the application of MXene-modified electrochemical sensors for the detection of specific drugs

Pages 171-182

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.540451.1016

Rahadian Zainul, Mustafa Mudhafar, Shakinaz Desa, Ahmed Abduljabbar Jaloob Aljanaby, Mohammad Mirzaei Heydari, Bahar Shirazipour, Solmaz Karimi

Abstract The remarkable qualities of MXene, a new family of two-dimensional transition metal carbides, such as its hydrophilicity, high electrical conductivity, huge surface area, and attractive topological structure, have made it a potential material for a number of uses. MXenes are efficient transducers for electrochemical sensing because they can dissolve in ordinary solvents and combine with other nanomaterials to produce composites. According to the literature, MXenes are increasingly being used in the detection of various analytes. An overview of the applications of MXene-modified materials is provided for sensing applications in this study. The analytes are grouped, and a thorough explanation of each category is provided to facilitate the audience’s understanding.

Nanocarriers in medicine: A review of current advances and clinical applications

Pages 183-193

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.535128.1010

Razieh Razavi

Abstract It's becoming increasingly clear that nanocarriers are fundamentally changing how we approach modern medicine. By allowing for incredibly precise drug delivery, they're making treatments more effective while dramatically reducing those dreaded side effects. These sophisticated tiny systems, which operate on the scale of 1 to 1000 nanometers, work by encapsulating therapeutic compounds. This shields them from breaking down too early, improves their solubility, and lets doctors control exactly when and where they're released. This review takes a deep dive into the different types of nanocarriers out there, from organic to inorganic and even hybrid materials, and explores how they're being used to fight cancer, infections, and to aid in tissue regeneration and diagnostics. We also tackle the big hurdles—like making sure they're biocompatible, figuring out how to manufacture them at scale, and navigating the regulatory maze—while also spotlighting the coolest new advances, like stimuli-responsive carriers and designs that mimic our body's own extracellular matrix. Finally, we'll look at what the future might hold, including wider clinical use and truly personalized nanomedicine.

Bisphenol a: Analysis using modified graphite screen printed electrode as an electrochemical sensor

Pages 194-202

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.537230.1013

Mahdi Adeli Nejad, Hamid Sarhadi, Erfan Sarhadi

Abstract The La2O3/Co3O4 nanocomposite modified graphite screen printed electrode (La2O3/Co3O4/GSPE) was used to create an electrochemical sensor for the electrochemical measurement of bisphenol A. Through the use of SEM, the La2O3/Co3O4 nanocomposite was characterized. Voltammetry was employed to study the electrochemical properties of bisphenol A. The electrode modification caused a notable shift in the peak potential (~220 mV) and a significant rise in the anodic peak current (~2.5 times). The oxidation peak current of bisphenol A fluctuated linearly with concentration under ideal circumstances, spanning a broad range of 0.5 to 450.0 µM. The detection limit of this technique was as low as 0.15 µM. The proposed technique was also successfully applied to identify bisphenol A in actual samples.

Functionalized graphene enhances irinotecan binding: An all-atom MD study of –OH/–COOH decorated surfaces

Pages 203-219

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.549657.1018

Arefeh Esmaeili, Mahshid Ahmadi, Zahra Keshavarz

Abstract The development of efficient nanocarriers for anticancer therapeutics remains a major challenge due to limitations in drug solubility, bioavailability, and systemic toxicity. In this work, all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were employed to investigate the adsorption behavior of the chemotherapeutic agent irinotecan on pristine graphene (GRP) and functionalized graphene (GRP-F) decorated with hydroxyl (–OH) and carboxyl (–COOH) groups. Both systems were simulated for 100 ns under explicit solvent conditions using the CHARMM36 force field. Energy analyses revealed that drug binding to pristine graphene was predominantly driven by van der Waals interactions, whereas the functionalized surface provided additional stabilization through electrostatic interactions and intermittent hydrogen bonding. Root mean square deviation (RMSD) and radius of gyration (Rg) analyses confirmed the stable adsorption of irinotecan on both carriers, with reduced conformational fluctuations in the GRP-F complex. Contact number and center-of-mass distance profiles demonstrated tighter and more persistent drug–carrier associations in the functionalized system. Moreover, solvent accessible surface area (SASA) analysis indicated reduced solvent exposure upon adsorption, while radial distribution functions (RDF) highlighted a significantly higher probability density of irinotecan near GRP-F compared with pristine graphene. Overall, these findings demonstrate that surface functionalization enhances the binding affinity and stability of irinotecan adsorption on graphene, providing a more favorable platform for drug loading and retention. This study offers atomistic-level insights into graphene-based nanocarriers and underscores the potential of functionalized graphene for the design of next-generation delivery systems for chemotherapeutic agents.

Bimetallic nanoparticle of yttrium-copper metal-organic framework (Y-Cu-MOF) as a novel modifier for determination of ascorbic acid

Pages 220-232

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2025.551291.1019

Shamsi Esmaeelzadeh, Niloufar Akbarzadeh-T

Abstract A solvothermal synthesis approach was used to fabricate a bimetallic metal-organic framework (MOF) based on yttrium and copper (Y-Cu MOF). The formed material was characterized extensively using several techniques including field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and elemental mapping. Subsequently, a voltammetric platform for the selective detection of ascorbic acid (ASA) was generated from the Y-Cu MOF nanostructure by modifying a screen-printed electrode (SPE) (Y-Cu-MOFs/SPE). A detailed electrochemical evaluation was also conducted using a wide range of electroanalytical techniques for the modified electrode and its response against ASA. The electrochemical results using the modified electrode demonstrated a large increase in current response associated with the oxidation of ASA, benefiting from the facilitating properties of the Y-Cu-MOFs. The Y-Cu-MOFs/CPE sensor also verified great sensitivity toward the detection of ASA within a linear detection range (0.4-500.0 μM) and a detection limit that was as low as 0.1±0.01 μM, when operated under the optimized conditions. The obtained results also verified practical analysis with recovery rates of ASA ranging from 97.1% to 102.0%. on the Y-Cu-MOFs/CPE electrochemical sensor in both the ASA injection samples and urine.
