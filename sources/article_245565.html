Molecular dynamics simulation and conformational stability analysis of HPV-16 E6 oncoprotein–ligand complexes: cilostazol as a promising stable candidate

Document Type : Original Article

Author

Mahshid Ahmadi

Department of Chemistry, Graduate University of Advanced Technology, Kerman, Iran

https://doi.org/10.48309/ajnm.2026.584345.1041
Abstract
Persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus type 16 (HPV-16) is a primary driver of cervical cancer and several other malignancies. The viral E6 oncoprotein promotes oncogenesis by mediating the degradation of the tumor suppressor p53. Therefore, compounds capable of maintaining stable interactions with E6 may provide a rational basis for antiviral and anticancer drug development. In this study, the dynamic stability of five selected ligands (axitinib, cabozantinib, tivozanib, deflazacort, and cilostazol) in complex with HPV-16 E6 was evaluated through 300-ns all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations using GROMACS 2022.4, the CHARMM36 force field, and the TIP3P water model. Structural stability, flexibility, compactness, and interaction persistence were assessed using RMSD, RMSF, hydrogen bonding, radius of gyration (Rg), protein–ligand distance, contact number, and solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) analyses. Among the five complexes, the cilostazol–E6 system exhibited the most favorable overall dynamic stability profile throughout the 300-ns simulation. It maintained 6–10 persistent hydrogen bonds, a stable radius of gyration (~1.70 nm), a consistent protein–ligand distance (~0.18 nm), and a protein–ligand contact profile that increased from approximately 800–1200 contacts during the early stage to 1500–2000 contacts during the later phase of the simulation, along with an average SASA of approximately 97 nm², collectively reflecting sustained structural integrity and interaction persistence. In comparison, the other ligands (axitinib, cabozantinib, tivozanib, and deflazacort) generally exhibited less favorable dynamic behavior across the evaluated molecular dynamics parameters, indicating lower overall complex stability. These findings demonstrate that cilostazol forms a more stable complex with HPV-16 E6 than the other tested compounds and represents a promising candidate for further experimental investigation as a potential E6-targeted antiviral and anticancer agent.
