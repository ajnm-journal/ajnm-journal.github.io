Nanocarriers in medicine: A review of current advances and clinical applications

Razavi, Razieh

Read full-text PDFResolve DOIPublisher record

Abstract

It's becoming increasingly clear that nanocarriers are fundamentally changing how we approach modern medicine. By allowing for incredibly precise drug delivery, they're making treatments more effective while dramatically reducing those dreaded side effects. These sophisticated tiny systems, which operate on the scale of 1 to 1000 nanometers, work by encapsulating therapeutic compounds. This shields them from breaking down too early, improves their solubility, and lets doctors control exactly when and where they're released. This review takes a deep dive into the different types of nanocarriers out there, from organic to inorganic and even hybrid materials, and explores how they're being used to fight cancer, infections, and to aid in tissue regeneration and diagnostics. We also tackle the big hurdles—like making sure they're biocompatible, figuring out how to manufacture them at scale, and navigating the regulatory maze—while also spotlighting the coolest new advances, like stimuli-responsive carriers and designs that mimic our body's own extracellular matrix. Finally, we'll look at what the future might hold, including wider clinical use and truly personalized nanomedicine.
