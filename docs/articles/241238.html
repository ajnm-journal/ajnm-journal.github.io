Application of metal-organic framework-based nanocomposites as drug delivery systems for doxorubicin anticancer drug

Elzainy, Ahmed; Alshhab, Abdallah; Jaradat, Hekmah M; Alokour, Mamoon; Abubaker, Mohamed Aamer

Read full-text PDFResolve DOIPublisher record

Abstract

Doxorubicin (DOX), a prominent chemotherapeutic agent from the anthracycline class, is extensively utilized in the management of diverse malignancies, including cervical carcinoma, osteosarcoma, and breast cancer. A major limitation of DOX is its lack of target specificity, leading to significant damage to healthy cells and resulting in a range of adverse side effects. A drug delivery system (DDS) is designed to transport pharmaceutical agents in a controlled and sustained manner, specifically to targeted sites within the body. Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) possess a suite of advantageous characteristics for drug delivery, including high design flexibility, precisely defined porosity, stimuli-responsive behavior, an exceptionally large surface area, and tunable morphology. To date, numerous MOF structures have been extensively investigated and employed as nanocarriers. Their significant appeal in the realm of drug delivery stems from their favorable biocompatibility and exceptional capacity for high drug loading. MOFs, particularly when integrated with complementary materials, have been widely explored as innovative carrier platforms for the delivery of DOX. DDSs function by releasing their active pharmaceutical ingredients in a manner designed to achieve a specific and optimal therapeutic outcome. MOFs serve as a versatile foundational platform for drug delivery, often integrated into nanocomposite materials such as MOF-polymer nanocomposites, MOF-metal oxide nanocomposites, and MOF-graphene oxide nanocomposites. Herein, a summary of MOF nanocomposite-based DDSs for the delivery of DOX is provided over the last eight years.
