Bimetallic nanoparticle of yttrium-copper metal-organic framework (Y-Cu-MOF) as a novel modifier for determination of ascorbic acid

Esmaeelzadeh, Shamsi; Akbarzadeh-T, Niloufar

Read full-text PDFResolve DOIPublisher record

Abstract

A solvothermal synthesis approach was used to fabricate a bimetallic metal-organic framework (MOF) based on yttrium and copper (Y-Cu MOF). The formed material was characterized extensively using several techniques including field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and elemental mapping. Subsequently, a voltammetric platform for the selective detection of ascorbic acid (ASA) was generated from the Y-Cu MOF nanostructure by modifying a screen-printed electrode (SPE) (Y-Cu-MOFs/SPE). A detailed electrochemical evaluation was also conducted using a wide range of electroanalytical techniques for the modified electrode and its response against ASA. The electrochemical results using the modified electrode demonstrated a large increase in current response associated with the oxidation of ASA, benefiting from the facilitating properties of the Y-Cu-MOFs. The Y-Cu-MOFs/CPE sensor also verified great sensitivity toward the detection of ASA within a linear detection range (0.4-500.0 μM) and a detection limit that was as low as 0.1±0.01 μM, when operated under the optimized conditions. The obtained results also verified practical analysis with recovery rates of ASA ranging from 97.1% to 102.0%. on the Y-Cu-MOFs/CPE electrochemical sensor in both the ASA injection samples and urine.
