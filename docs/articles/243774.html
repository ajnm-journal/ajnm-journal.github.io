Plant-mediated green synthesis and characterization of zinc oxide nanoparticle for enhanced antimicrobial activities against Escherichia coli

Aguree, Sylvenus; Dare, Nyefene Joe; Abagale, Samson Abah

Read full-text PDFResolve DOIPublisher record

Abstract

Medicinal plants have long been a crucial component of traditional medicine, retaining their importance in human health management for centuries. These plants act as a reservoir of bioactive compounds, which are known to possess antimicrobial properties. Despite the availability of various antimicrobial agents, concerns regarding antibiotic resistance remain prevalent. Several strategies are employed to address this issue. In this study, zinc oxide nanoparticles synthesized from Iccacina oliviformis were prepared using an eco-friendly precipitation method. The antimicrobial activities of both the aqueous extract and the synthesized nanoparticles were assessed using minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) methods. Characterization of the prepared zinc oxide nanoparticles was performed using Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Phytochemical analysis identified several constituents, including alkaloids, tannins, saponins, flavonoids, terpenoids, and anthraquinones. The ZnO NPs synthesized from Iccacina oliviformis demonstrated stronger antimicrobial activity, with an MIC of 0.98 µg/mL, compared to 7.8 µg/mL for the aqueous extract of Iccacina oliviformis root tuber alone. FTIR analysis revealed the presence of alcohols, carbonyls, alkenes, and nitro-compounds, phenols, functional groups originating from O-H, C=O, C–C, N=O, and C-H. The XRD analysis indicated a hexagonal wurtzite phase of ZnO, while SEM images displayed nano-sized structures characterized by agglomeration and high surface roughness. The formation of ZnO nanoparticles was clearly observed in this study, and antimicrobial assays confirmed that the synthesized ZnO nanoparticles exhibited inhibitory effects against Escherichia coli. Further research is needed to investigate the potential toxicities associated with the synthesized nanoparticles.
