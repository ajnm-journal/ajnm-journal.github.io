Electrochemical determination of medicinal drug gliclazide using carbon paste electrode modified with Fe3O4/GO nanocomposites

Irannezhad, Fatemeh; Seyed-Yazdi, Jamileh

Read full-text PDFResolve DOIPublisher record

Abstract

This study presents the solvothermal synthesis of a FeO₄@CuO core-shell nanostructure. The synthesized materials were extensively characterized to determine their chemical bonding, element composition, and macro, and microstructure. To characterize the nanostructures, advanced analytical characterization methods were used, including Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR), X-ray Diffraction (XRD), Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM), and Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy (FE-SEM/EDX). The successful synthesis of the core-shell structure was unequivocally confirmed through these characterization techniques, which also provided insight into the morphology of the synthesized nanostructures along with structural support. Afterward, an electrochemical sensor based on a modified carbon paste electrode, using FeO₄@CuO core-shell nanostructure, was developed for detecting gliclazide. The modified electrode showed effective behavior in gliclazide electrocatalysis, an experimental sensor created using Fe3O4/GO/CPE compared to bare CPE shows a significant decrease in peak potentials and an increase in sensor sensitivities. The sensor was used to detect gliclazide using differential pulse voltammetry. A detection limit of 0.03 µM (S/N=3) was identified, and a linear correspondence of response currents and gliclazide concentrations was found over the broader concentration range of 1.0-700.0 µM. The study findings suggest that Fe3O4/GO/CPE presents an effective voltametric sensor for future analyses. The sensors showed the ability to effectively detect gliclazide in real samples with high sensitivity.
